📝 Grant Proposal Narrative

Project Title

The Distomo Resilience Project: Restoring Spaces, Preserving Memory, and Supporting Community Healing

Need Statement

Communities shaped by trauma, conflict, displacement, violence, and historical grief often carry visible and invisible wounds. Neglected public spaces can deepen that sense of abandonment, while stigma around trauma can prevent survivors, veterans, families, and underserved groups from feeling seen, supported, or connected.

Proposed Solution

The Distomo Resilience Project responds by combining practical restoration work with trauma-informed storytelling and public education. Through painting, clean-up, beautification, creative workshops, digital mapping, and historical documentation, the project creates a shared process of renewal that honors both place and people.

Key Deliverables

  • Restore or beautify at least one community building or public space.
  • Create a before-and-after visual archive documenting the restoration process.
  • Develop a digital story map showing the project location, history, restoration work, and community impact.
  • Host or support trauma-informed creative reflection activities.
  • Publish a short public impact report for funders, sponsors, and community partners.

Long-Term Vision

The Distomo pilot will serve as a replicable model for future Missions & Mental Health projects that combine public service, survivor dignity, cultural preservation, mental health awareness, and community-based restoration.

🎨 Sample Workshop: Painting Our Resilience

Length: 90 minutes

Theme: Expressing past, present, and hope through color, shape, and shared reflection.

Step 1: Grounding & Connection

Begin with gentle breathing, a simple safety statement, and a non-verbal check-in using colors.

Step 2: Landscape of Emotion

Participants create three sections representing past, present, and future using abstract marks, colors, textures, and shapes.

Step 3: Finding the Bridge

Participants add lines, paths, patterns, or symbols that connect the three sections, modeling integration and movement forward.

Step 4: Reflection

Sharing is voluntary. The focus is not artistic skill, but meaning, safety, dignity, and the possibility of hope.
